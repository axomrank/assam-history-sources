Tezpur Rock Inscription Harjaravarman

Date: Gupta year 510, conventionally about 829–830 CE.
Ruler: Harjaravarman, Salastambha line.
Place: A rock beside the Brahmaputra at Tezpur.

SUMMARY
A damaged public record concerns a dispute and regulations affecting boats, boatmen or fishermen and officials. Earlier scholars interpreted it as a judicial settlement involving land; Sharma questions that reading and understands its central purpose as regulating where civilian boats could operate, with a payment for infringement.

HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
Rare evidence for river administration, official authority and economic life. It is valuable precisely because it differs from the usual religious land charter. The damaged text makes the detailed legal interpretation uncertain.

Source: M. M. Sharma, Inscriptions of Ancient Assam (1978), supplied PDF pages 136-142.
