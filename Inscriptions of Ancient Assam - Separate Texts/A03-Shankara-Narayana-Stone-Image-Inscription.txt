Shankara Narayana Stone Image Inscription

Date: Eighth century CE in the edition.
Ruler: Sri Jivara in Sharma’s reading; exact dynastic placement is interpretive.
Place: Deopani; the larger of two inscribed composite Shiva–Vishnu images discussed in the book.

SUMMARY
A three-line Sanskrit inscription on a composite image refers to the reigning monarch and to Shankara and Narayana. Sharma discusses whether the words name the image or refer to prayers recited during its consecration. The text contains non-classical linguistic forms and difficult readings.

HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
Useful for religious image worship, royal names and early devotional practices. Sharma’s proposed succession links and interpretation of the consecration language are scholarly reconstructions, not explicit complete historical statements.

Source: M. M. Sharma, Inscriptions of Ancient Assam (1978), supplied PDF pages 364-365.
