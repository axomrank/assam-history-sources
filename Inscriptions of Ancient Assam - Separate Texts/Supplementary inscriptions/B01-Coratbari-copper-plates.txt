Coratbari copper plates

Date: Eleventh century CE; twelfth regnal year of Ratnapala
Ruler or named person: Ratnapala
Place: Conventionally Coratbari; Sharma could not establish how and when the plates entered the Assam State Museum.

SUMMARY
A three-plate grant gives land yielding 4,000 units of paddy, taken from Santidasa Pataka and Bhattaya Pataka in Havrnga visaya, to Siddhapala, son of Nidhipala and Dattayika and grandson of Jayapala. Sharma reads a city-name Hadapyaka and explicitly rejects an earlier report that the readable text named Hadapesvara.

HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
Adds a distinct Ratnapala charter to the two in the main corpus. It contains evidence for a city, beneficiary genealogy, agricultural assessment and land boundaries.

LIMITS OF THE EVIDENCE
A damaged record, presented only partly in the introduction. Twelfth regnal year is checked in the page image. The old CSV’s exact c.1021 date is withdrawn; accession chronology is uncertain. Some place-name spellings remain uncertain.

Source: M. M. Sharma, Inscriptions of Ancient Assam, Gauhati University, 1978; introduction pp. 0.21–0.24, supplied PDF pp. 38–41
