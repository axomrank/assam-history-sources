Puspabhadra Copper Plates Dharmapala

Date: Undated; assigned by Sharma to a later stage of Dharmapala’s reign, in the first half of the twelfth century.
Ruler: Dharmapala, Kamarupa Pala dynasty.
Place: Dry bed of the Puspabhadra river at North Guwahati; reached Hem Chandra Goswami in 1909.

SUMMARY
Dharmapala grants land assessed as yielding 10,000 units of paddy to the Brahmin Madhusudana, associated with the worship of Madhava. The charter opens with praise of Vishnu in his boar incarnation. Sharma credits the first eight verses to the king and the remainder to the poet Aniruddha.

HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
Notable for substantial royal religious patronage, its explicitly Vaishnava opening and evidence of courtly literary composition. Sharma’s later-reign placement is an inference rather than a date stated in the inscription.

Source: M. M. Sharma, Inscriptions of Ancient Assam (1978), supplied PDF pages 309-327.
