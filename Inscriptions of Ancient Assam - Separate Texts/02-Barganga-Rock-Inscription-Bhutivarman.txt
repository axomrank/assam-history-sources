Barganga Rock Inscription Bhutivarman

Date: Sixth century CE; precise dating is disputed.
Ruler: Bhutivarman, named as the reigning king.
Place: Beside the Barganga river, in the region described in the book as Nowgong district.

SUMMARY
This short rock record is associated with a ruined religious establishment. Sharma interprets it as a religious foundation made by an official or district governor for the king’s welfare and longevity. The inscription is difficult to read, and proposed readings of its date differ.

HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
It supplies contemporary evidence for Bhutivarman’s rule and for religious patronage through local officials. A precise year or a detailed reconstruction of the damaged wording should not be treated as certain.

Source: M. M. Sharma, Inscriptions of Ancient Assam (1978), supplied PDF pages 58-63.
