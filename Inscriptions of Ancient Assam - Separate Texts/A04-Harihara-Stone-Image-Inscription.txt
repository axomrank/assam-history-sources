Harihara Stone Image Inscription

Date: Eighth century CE in the edition.
Ruler: Diglekhavarman in Sharma’s reading.
Place: Deopani; the smaller of two inscribed composite Shiva–Vishnu images discussed in the book.

SUMMARY
A short three-line inscription records an image of Harihara given during the reign of Maharajadhiraja Diglekhavarman to the son of Kalhiya, accompanied by the recitation of a hundred names of Hari, according to Sharma’s translation. The Sanskrit has irregular forms and some names are uncertain.

HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE
Evidence for composite Shiva–Vishnu worship, image donation and devotional recitation. The suggestion that this ruler immediately followed Sri Jivara is an interpretation and should not be treated as established succession.

Source: M. M. Sharma, Inscriptions of Ancient Assam (1978), supplied PDF pages 366-366.
