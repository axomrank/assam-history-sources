KAMARUPA LANDSCAPE EXPLORER — RESEARCH AND DRAWING NOTES
Prepared 1 November 2026

OPEN
Double-click Kamarupa-Explorer.html. The map, source summaries, region selection
and inscription overlay work offline. Keep maps and atlas-assets beside the HTML.
Existing local source PDFs and TXT files are linked from the Sources dialog.
The Neog and Kakati reference links are online and require internet access.

WHAT THIS EXPLORER SHOWS
There are three source perspectives:
1. Four pithas: an illustrative landscape following Dimbeswar Neog's sequence.
2. Kalika Purana: an illustration of the broader textual reach described in 50.76–77.
3. Yogini Tantra: geographic reference anchors plus direction cards for unresolved
   references, based on the existing local working translation.

These are not successive dated political frontiers. There is no ruler/year slider,
area calculation or asserted conversion of yojanas into kilometres.

FOUR PITHAS: DEFAULT READING
Source: Dimbeswar Neog, Introduction to Assam, printed pp.59–60,
section “Ratna, Kama, Suvarna and Saumar Pitha Divisions”.
Reference scan: https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/34806.pdf

West to east:
Ratnapitha: Karatoya — Swarna-kosha / Sankosh.
Kamapitha: Sankosh — Rupika / Rupahi, in the Nagaon area.
Suvarnapitha: Rupahi — Bhairabi / Bharali, at Tezpur.
Saumarapitha: Bharali — Dikrang, in the Lakhimpur subdivision.

The last limit in this reading is NOT automatically substituted with Dibang at
Sadiya. Dikrong and Dibang are different modern rivers. The modern Dikrong line
is an approximate geographic aid for Neog's Lakhimpur reference.

ALTERNATE FOUR-REGION READING
Source: Banikanta Kakati, The Mother Goddess Kamakhya, printed pp.7–8.
Online text: https://pubhtml5.com/iuzeb/wsim/basic/
Kakati reports a four-region division in the later Haragaurisamvada:
Ratna: Karatoya–Svarnakosa; Kama: Svarnakosa–Kapili;
Svarna: Puspika–Bhairavi; Saumara: Bhairavi–Dikrang.
This is a reported reading, not a fresh collation of that Sanskrit text.
The differences are displayed in the comparison table, not silently merged.

KALIKA PURANA
Local source: Kalika-Purana-B-N-Shastri-Surendra-Pratap-2008.pdf and .txt.
50.76–77, printed p.353: the region east of Karatoya up to Dikkaravasini is
called Kamarupa; triangular, thirty yojanas broad and a hundred long, containing
many hills and rivers. Compare the Naraka narrative at 38.118 and 38.127,
printed pp.245–246, which uses Lalitakanta as an eastern reference.
The exact modern identification of Lalitakanta is not fixed in this explorer.
These passages are not the source of the four internal boundaries shown in
the default later-tradition view.

YOGINI TANTRA
Local source: Yogini-Tantra-English-Working-Translation-2026.txt,
based on the Mishra edition (1984); original PDF remains in this collection.
I.11.16–18 and 21–22: Kanjagiri in the north, Karatoya to the west, Diksu to
the east, Brahmaputra–Laksha confluence to the south; also Dikkaravasini and a
triangular extent. Compare the more complex sacred-seat descriptions in II.1.
The English text is a working translation; the geographic readings retain its
qualifications. This explorer does not independently establish the modern
identities of Kanjagiri, Diksu or the historical southern confluence.

CARTOGRAPHIC METHOD — READ BEFORE REUSING SHAPES
All coloured region polygons and the dashed outer enclosure are editorial
illustrations made for this interface. They are not digitised from an ancient
map or derived from a boundary survey. Connecting seams and north–south
edges are not furnished by the cited passages.
The outer enclosure suggests broader reach; it is not identical to the
four-colour region sequence. Its southern shape is especially schematic.
Karatoya's historical course is not reconstructed. The dotted reference stroke
for Karatoya and the short Rupahi stroke are explanatory sketches.
The Teesta/Karatoya combined line in the copied map collection is deliberately
not used as an established historical Karatoya course.
Sankosh, Bharali and Dikrong highlights use the copied modern-reference lines;
their ancient courses and role as full territorial boundaries are not established.
The Karatoya and Sadiya-area/Dikkaravasini markers are broad reference anchors,
not surveyed ancient points. Kamakhya is a modern Nilachal locality reference,
not a plotted ancient royal capital.
Kanjagiri, Diksu and the southern confluence appear in separate direction cards,
not as map points. No geographic coordinates are assigned to these three
unresolved references in the interpretation data.

INSCRIPTIONS
The optional layer contains the 28 records marked 'within' the 350–1100 catalogue
whose reference coordinates lie within the displayed landscape's regional bounds
(latitude 23–29N; longitude 88–96.5E). These are catalogue reference points, not
all known inscriptions of Kamarupa. Dashed markers preserve locality uncertainty.
Nearby points are grouped at the current zoom. Individual selections open their
summary and link to the full inscription explorer. No pitha is assigned by polygon.
The source CSV and its exact Barpanara/Deopani user-supplied coordinate values
remain unchanged. See Location-Column-Notes.txt for their provenance.
